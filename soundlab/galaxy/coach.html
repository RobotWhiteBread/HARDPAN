0. Hit DNA: what the research says, the numbers by style, and how to break the pattern

Intros shrank 4x

Year-end US top 10, 1986 to 2015 (303 songs): instrumental intros fell from about 20 seconds in the mid-80s to about 5, and tempos rose.
Move: the voice or the hook by bar 5. At your tempo that is -.
Léveillé Gauvin, Musicae Scientiae 2017

Minor took over

1,010 Billboard year-end hits, 1965 to 2009: major-key songs fell from 85% to 42.5%, tempos slowed, and fast songs in minor keys ("mixed cues") became common.
Break it: the crowded corner is fast and minor. Slow and major, or Dorian (minor with a bright 6th), stands out.
Schellenberg and von Scheve 2012

Everything sounds the same

Fifty years of pop measured: narrower pitch moves, a more uniform palette of timbres, and rising loudness.
Your edge: timbre is the least crowded axis left. A sound nobody else owns (your own recordings) is novelty the math says listeners notice.
Serrà et al., Scientific Reports 2012

Loud doesn't win anymore

Spotify turns every track to -14 LUFS integrated (ITU 1770) and asks for true peak under -1 dB. A master crushed to -7 LUFS just gets turned down 7 dB, minus its punch.
Move: keep the crest factor (peak minus RMS) healthy; that is the punch slider below.
Spotify for Artists
