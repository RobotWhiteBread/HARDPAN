HOW THE SOUND GALAXY WORKS

Every file is opened and measured by soundlab's analyzer: the first 30 seconds, mixed to mono, cut into 2048-sample windows (Hann, hop 512) and turned into a spectrum. Nothing is uploaded and no AI model listens to your files: every number below is plain signal math you can check. Full details are in METHODS.md next to this page.

What each axis measures

BrightnessSpectral centroid: the balance point of the average spectrum, in Hz, on a log scale. Low = dark, high = bright. Tags: dark under 900 Hz, bright over 3.5 kHz.
PunchCrest factor: peak level minus average (RMS) level, in dB. High = spiky transients, low = squashed or sustained. Tagged punchy at 18 dB or more with a fast attack, squashed under 9 dB.
Low endShare of all energy between 20 and 250 Hz (sub 20 to 60 plus low 60 to 250). Tagged subby when over 25% sits under 60 Hz.
LengthDuration in seconds, on a log scale.
NoisinessSpectral flatness: how even the spectrum is (geometric mean over arithmetic mean). Near 0 = pitched and tonal, near 1 = noise. Tagged noisy over 0.25.
Stereo widthSide level over mid level ((L-R)/2 against (L+R)/2). 0 = mono. Tagged wide over 0.35. Mono files sit at the bottom.
SnapHow fast the sound reaches its loudest moment (attack time, about 12 ms resolution). Higher = snappier.
TempoFor sounds 1.8 s or longer: autocorrelation of the onset envelope (spectral flux), tested from 60 to 180 BPM, with a bonus when the file is a whole number of bars. The REAPER project tools prefer a tempo written in the file name when there is one.

How positions are scaled

On "Choose the axes", each axis is a rank, not a raw value: the middle of an axis is the median sound in your library, and the ends are your darkest and brightest (or least and most punchy) sounds. Ranking keeps sounds from piling up against an edge. Some measures produce big ties: about 1 in 5 sounds (mostly hats) has no measurable energy under 250 Hz, and half of all sounds reach their peak in the first window. Inside a tie, sounds are ordered by a related measure (low end ties by brightness, brightest at the edge; snap ties by punch, punchiest at the edge), so the tie shows as a band whose thickness is its size. Mono files all share one stereo width and still sit on one plane. The shadows on the walls are the cloud seen straight on from each side: brighter means more sounds stacked behind that spot. The bars in the side panel use the same ranks, so 80% means "more than 80% of your library".

Sound types view

Each sound type gets its own box. With "Measured axes" (the default), each box uses three measures that matter for that kind of sound, ranked within that type only, so the middle of the kick box is a typical kick, not a typical sound. Kicks, snares, perc and bass: brightness, punch or length. Hats: brightness, length (closed to open) and noisiness. Melodic: brightness, length, noisiness. Loops: tempo, brightness, punch. Textures and FX: noisiness, length, width or brightness. The axis names are written on every box. Focus a box (double-click) to list its islands: each shows its size, a short profile on the three axes, its main packs, and its most typical sound (the member closest to the island's average), which plays when you hover.

Similarity islands

Each sound becomes a point in 7 dimensions (the measures above except tempo, scaled 0 to 1 between the 1st and 99th percentile), plus a weight for its sound type so islands stay mostly one kind of sound. K-means groups them into 360 islands. t-SNE then places the 360 island centers in 3D so similar islands sit near each other, and each sound is spread around its island center along the three main ways sounds in that island differ. Distance means difference; the directions themselves have no fixed meaning. Island names are the main sound type plus any tag shared by at least 30% of its members.

Sound types and tags

Types are rule-based guesses from length, where the energy sits, flatness and how many hits a file has. Loops: 1.8 s or longer, with at least 4 hits, more than 1.2 hits a second, and a whole number of bars or more than 4 s. One-shots under 1.2 s: kick if at least half the energy is under 250 Hz and the centroid is under 900 Hz; hat if at least half is over 2.5 kHz and it is noisy; snare or clap if at least 30% sits between 250 Hz and 2.5 kHz; otherwise perc. Longer sounds: bass, melodic, texture or FX and noise by low-end share and flatness. Lofi means 85% of the energy sits below 4.5 kHz.

Key, note and matching

Key: a 12-note chroma profile (55 Hz to 5 kHz) correlated with the Krumhansl-Kessler major and minor profiles, only for tonal sounds and only when one key clearly wins. Note: harmonic product spectrum on the first 1.5 s of sounds up to 6 s long. Match key and tempo keeps keys that are the same, a fifth apart, or relative major and minor, and tempos within 6% (or at double or half time).

Neighbors, copies, gaps, use

  • Sounds like this: the 14 nearest sounds by distance across the 7 ranked measures, plus tempo difference when both have a tempo. Exact copies are skipped.
  • Extra copies: files with identical length, level, peak, brightness and sample rate, usually the same sample shipped in more than one pack.
  • Gaps: the chosen axes are cut into a 6 x 6 x 6 grid. A gap is a cell where your library has at least 25 sounds but 2 or fewer of the chosen type, next to cells that do have that type.
  • Used vs owned: every REAPER project (.rpp) in your project folders is read, and each sample it references is matched by exact file path.

Limits

Types, keys and tempos are educated guesses, not ground truth: key detection on drums is unreliable, a long one-shot can read as a loop, and only the first 30 s of a long file is measured. The measures describe the sound, not whether it is good. Use the map to find candidates, then trust your ears.
